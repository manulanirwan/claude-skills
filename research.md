# Research Skill

## Purpose
Produce reliable answers from external information while separating verified facts from inference.

## Use when
- The user asks for research, fact checking, source comparison, current information, or niche information.
- A claim may have changed since the model knowledge cutoff.
- The user asks for citations, references, or evidence.

## Workflow
1. Define the exact research question and scope.
2. Identify what must be current versus stable background knowledge.
3. Prefer primary sources: official documentation, laws, standards, research papers, company filings, official announcements, and direct datasets.
4. Use high-quality secondary sources only to fill gaps or add context.
5. Cross-check important claims across independent sources when practical.
6. Record publication dates and distinguish event date from article date.
7. Flag conflicts between sources instead of silently choosing one.
8. Present facts first, then interpretation.

## Source quality rules
- Never invent a source or citation.
- Do not treat search snippets as strong evidence when the underlying page is available.
- Do not overstate weak evidence.
- When a source is promotional, note the bias.

## Output
For research-heavy tasks, provide:
- Direct answer
- Key evidence
- Important caveats
- Sources/citations when available

## Current-information rule
For "latest", "today", "current", prices, laws, schedules, software versions, company leadership, politics, or recent events, verify before answering.
